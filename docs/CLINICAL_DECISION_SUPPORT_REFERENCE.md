### Cumulative Striatal $D_2$ Exposure Strata
- **< 200 mg CPZ eq**: Low / Augmentation dosing ($<60\%$ $D_2$ occupancy). Effective for adjunctive MDD or GAD, subtherapeutic for acute schizophrenia.
- **200–600 mg CPZ eq**: Optimal Therapeutic Window ($65\%–80\%$ $D_2$ occupancy). Maximizes antipsychotic efficacy while minimizing parkinsonian extrapyramidal symptoms.
- **600–1000 mg CPZ eq**: High-Dose Stratum ($>80\%$ $D_2$ occupancy). Substantially elevated liability for acute akathisia, dystonia, motor retardation, and hyperprolactinemia.
- **> 1000 mg CPZ eq**: Supratherapeutic Ceiling Alert. Significant long-term liability for Tardive Dyskinesia, secondary negative symptoms, cognitive blunting, and Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS).

## 2. Lithium Therapeutic Drug Monitoring (TDM) & Trough Kinetics

### 12-Hour Serum Trough Timing Window
- **Optimal Steady-State Trough**: Blood must be drawn **10 to 14 hours** post-evening dose after $\ge 5$ days on a stable regimen ($\sim 5$ elimination half-lives).
- **Premature Blood Draw (< 10 hours)**: Blood drawn during early gastrointestinal absorption and distribution phases. Falsely elevates serum lithium level by **20% to 40%**. Down-titrating based on premature draws risks clinical destabilization and manic relapse.
- **Delayed Blood Draw (> 14 hours)**: Underestimates standard trough due to continuous renal clearance.

### Linear Steady-State Titration Math
$$\text{New Dose (mg/day)} = \text{Current Daily Dose} \times \left(\frac{\text{Target Serum Level (mEq/L)}}{\text{Current Measured Level (mEq/L)}}\right)$$
*Adjust to the nearest commercially available capsule size (150 mg, 300 mg, or 450 mg ER).*

### Clinical Target Reference Ranges
- **Acute Mania / Mixed Episode**: $0.8\text{--}1.2\text{ mEq/L}$
- **Bipolar Maintenance / Suicide Prevention**: $0.6\text{--}0.8\text{ mEq/L}$
- **Unipolar Depression Augmentation**: $0.5\text{--}0.8\text{ mEq/L}$
- **Geriatric / Renal Frailty**: $0.4\text{--}0.6\text{ mEq/L}$

### The Lethal Collision Quartet (Acute Lithium Retention)
1. **NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Naproxen, Indomethacin, Celecoxib)**: Inhibit renal prostacyclin and PGE2 synthesis $\to$ reduces GFR $\to$ surges serum lithium by **30% to 60%**. *(Aspirin and Sulindac do not exhibit this magnitude of effect).*
2. **Thiazide Diuretics (Hydrochlorothiazide, Chlorthalidone)**: Block sodium reabsorption in distal convoluted tubule $\to$ induces compensatory proximal tubular sodium and lithium reabsorption $\to$ **30% to 50% level surge within 5–7 days**.
3. **ACE Inhibitors & ARBs (Lisinopril, Losartan, Enalapril)**: Dilate efferent glomerular arterioles $\to$ reduces intraglomerular hydrostatic filtration pressure $\to$ delayed lithium toxicity over 1–3 weeks.
4. **Volume Depletion / Hyponatremia**: Dehydration, febrile illness, vomiting, or sodium-restricted diets trigger intense proximal renal retention of Lithium.

### Toxicity Interventions & Emergent Hemodialysis Criteria
- **1.5–2.0 mEq/L (Moderate Toxicity)**: Hold Lithium, check BMP/eGFR, infuse isotonic $0.9\%$ IV normal saline at 150–200 mL/h to restore sodium balance and facilitate renal elimination.
- **2.0–2.5 mEq/L (Severe Toxicity)**: Neurologic signs (coarse tremor, ataxia, dysarthria, hyperreflexia). Inpatient medical telemetry, forced diuresis with saline.
- **> 2.5 mEq/L with symptoms / renal failure OR > 4.0 mEq/L unconditional**: Mandatory emergent hemodialysis.

## 3. Clozapine REMS Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) & Safety Protocol

### Risk Stratification & Monitoring Directives
- **General Population**:
  - **Normal ($\ge 1500/\mu\text{L}$)**: Continue therapy. Cadence: Weekly $\times 6$ months $\to$ Q2W $\times 6$ months $\to$ Q4W indefinitely.
  - **Mild Neutropenia ($1000\text{--}1499/\mu\text{L}$)**: Continue therapy. Increase ANC monitoring to **3 times per week** until $\ge 1500/\mu\text{L}$.
  - **Severe Neutropenia / Agranulocytosis ($< 1000/\mu\text{L}$)**: **IMMEDIATELY INTERRUPT CLOZAPINE**. Do not rechallenge without specialized REMS panel authorization. Daily ANC until recovery.
- **Benign Ethnic Neutropenia (BEN)**:
  - **Normal ($\ge 1000/\mu\text{L}$)**: Continue regular monitoring.
  - **Mild Neutropenia ($500\text{--}999/\mu\text{L}$)**: Continue therapy; monitor 3 times weekly.
  - **Severe Neutropenia ($< 500/\mu\text{L}$)**: **IMMEDIATELY INTERRUPT CLOZAPINE**.

### Critical Bedside Clinical Pearls
- **$\ge 48$-Hour Interruption Tolerance Reset**: If Clozapine is omitted for 48 hours or longer, tolerance to $\alpha_1$-adrenergic and $H_1$ receptor blockade collapses. Resuming at previous maintenance doses risks fatal orthostatic hypotension, syncope, and respiratory collapse. **Mandatory retitration from 12.5 mg or 25 mg daily.**
- **Smoking Cessation Surge**: Cigarette smoke hydrocarbons induce hepatic CYP1A2. Quitting smoking doubles Clozapine serum concentrations at the same dose within 48–72 hours, triggering grand mal seizures and toxic encephalopathy. Reduce dose by 30%–50% upon smoking cessation.
- **Gastrointestinal Hypomotility**: Muscarinic $M_3$ blockade causes severe constipation and paralytic ileus in up to 30% of patients; gastrointestinal mortality equals or exceeds agranulocytosis mortality. Proactive bowel regimens (PEG 3350, Docusate) are mandatory.

### Metformin Augmentation Threshold
Initiate Metformin (500 mg daily with evening meal $\to$ titrate to 1000 mg BID with meals as tolerated; confirm eGFR $\ge 30$) upon:
1. Weight gain $\ge 5\%$ of baseline body weight within first 3 months of SGA initiation.
2. Prediabetes diagnosis (Fasting plasma glucose 100–125 mg/dL or HbA1c 5.7%–6.4%).
